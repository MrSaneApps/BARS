### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces BARS (Blinded AI Review System), an automated peer-review pipeline that strips identifying metadata (titles, author names, institutions, journal names, publication dates, retraction notices, and repository handles) from full-text JATS XML/HTML manuscripts before evaluating them with an isolated language model (`gemini-4-argon`, web search disabled) on a pre-specified 10-point rubric (Methodological Rigor: 3.5, Claim-Evidence Alignment: 2.5, Biological Plausibility: 2.0, Reproducibility: 2.0; plus the 8-point Reproducibility-free Scientific Core, $\text{Core}_8$). Across six experiments totaling 1,874 reviews of 1,714 unique papers, the authors report that BARS separates 50 verified Sound Controls ($8.61 \pm 0.93$) from 50 retracted/refuted Flawed Controls ($4.42 \pm 1.97$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.97$, rising to $0.99$ after excluding 6 upstream database/simulation flaws), removes a $+0.35$-point unblinded prestige halo on routine papers, and achieves a single-run intraclass correlation of $\text{ICC}(2,1) = 0.86$ on routine papers ($0.97$ pooled across $30 \times 3 = 90$ runs; $r = 0.95$ across 29 natural duplicate draws). Applying BARS to 320 open-access articles from 2020–2025 across eight fields finds that $54.1\%$ ($173/320$) score below $5.0/10$. Across 50 matched preprint-to-published pairs and 100 open-review papers (261 human referees), revision at selective flagship and society journals improves manuscripts more than twice as much as revision at open-access megajournals ($+0.38$ vs. $+0.18$ $\text{Core}_8$ points; $+0.62$ vs. $+0.30/10$), and BARS recalls $97.9\%$ ($95/97$) of unresolved human critiques while identifying $3.85$ additional verified text/table flaws per paper. Across 915 date-blinded papers from 1991–2025, mean scores decline from $7.61$ in 1991–1995 to $5.79$ in 2021–2025 ($-0.58$ points/decade overall vs. $-0.23$ points/decade within recurring journals), and blinded self-audits of 238 career papers across two senior authors uncover concrete table, unit, and statistical errors in their own work. Code, prompts, SHA-256 input hashes, and review outputs are archived openly.

The study is an ambitious, well-organized meta-scientific audit backed by concrete forensic examples (such as ELISA concentrations exceeding $100\%$ of tissue weight, negative $p$-values, floating-point machine zero $9.88 \times 10^{-17}$ interpreted as a keystone taxon, and PAML `dN/dS = 99` error codes plotted as literal selection). However, five methodological and reporting issues require correction: (1) post-hoc relabeling of a failed Phase-1 Pilot control (`PMC8135569`, $4.2/10$) from Cohort B to Cohort A to reach the headline $100\%$ ($50/50$) specificity claim; (2) XML parser asymmetry across experiments (`<body>` only in Experiments 5 and 6a vs. `<body> + <back> + <custom-meta>` in Experiments 4 and 6b) while still reporting 10-point total scores that include the 2-point Data Availability/Reproducibility sub-score; (3) reliance on a single model (`gemini-4-argon`) both to generate Stage 1 reviews and to adjudicate Stage 2 concordance against human referees; (4) an undefined heuristic imputation of "Estimated Study Cost" (`Est. Study Cost`, Table S6) across 854 non-NIH-funded papers; and (5) cross-section numerical discrepancies (such as mismatched standard deviations across all seven 5-year windows between line 123 and Table S4, un-updated pre-expansion totals in Table S6 and Supplementary Sections S1–S3, and an omitted result for the promised self-audit of this manuscript itself on line 152). All of these issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis, transparent reporting of the unedited pilot labels, and text/table synchronization without generating new data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1 — BARS achieves 100% specificity ($<5.0$) on 50 Sound Controls, 76%–91% sensitivity on internally flawed papers ($\text{AUC} = 0.97\text{--}0.99$), removes a $+0.35$-point prestige halo, and reaches $\text{ICC}(2,1) = 0.86\text{--}0.97$ repeatability: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The separation between Sound and Flawed Controls ($8.61 \pm 0.93$ vs. $4.42 \pm 1.97$, $p = 9.2 \times 10^{-16}$), the 75-paper blinded vs. unblinded comparison ($-0.35$ points on Everyday Papers; $+1.19$ points on Flawed Controls when retraction headers are hidden), and the test-retest repeatability across 30 papers $\times$ 3 runs plus 29 natural cross-cohort replicates ($r = 0.951$) are quantitatively solid. However, Supplementary Table S1 (row 2 and row 7, `Original Pilot Label (PMC8135569 in B)`) reveals that during the Phase-1 Pilot (`ANON_01--75`), `PMC8135569` (`SP_ANON_003`, a middle-author paper by `[SENIOR_AUTHOR_1]` that scored $4.2/10$; Table S5 line 545) was originally assigned to Cohort B (Sound Controls), yielding $\widehat{\text{Sp}}_{<5.0} = 96.0\%$ ($24/25$) in the pilot and $98.0\%$ ($50/51$) overall. Reassigning `PMC8135569` from Cohort B to Cohort A after observing its pilot failure inflates Cohort B specificity at $<5.0$ to the unqualified $100\%$ ($50/50$) headline claim in the Abstract and main text. Additionally, the external replication on Brian et al. (2026) (Table S7, Youden's $J = 0.816$) rests on an illustrative $n = 3$ trial pairs (`T01`–`T03`), matching the small sample size for which the authors critique `Opus 5.5` and `Fable 5` in the same benchmark.

* **Claim 2 — Across 320 everyday open-access articles (2020–2025) from eight fields, 54.1% ($173/320$) fail on core rigor ($<5.0$), corresponding to an estimated 60%–70% true defect prevalence: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Programmatic sampling via Europe PMC queries ($n = 40$ per field across five in-domain and three out-of-distribution fields) and the observed gradient between quantitative model-based fields (Molecular Evolution $6.20 \pm 1.88$, Viral Evolution $6.19 \pm 1.44$) and template-heavy or unvalidated docking fields (Single-Cell Transcriptomics $4.13 \pm 1.76$, AI Drug Discovery $3.91 \pm 1.89$) are well documented. Two caveats temper the population-level prevalence extrapolation ($\hat{\pi}_{\text{RG}} = 60\%\text{--}70\%$, Eq. 3): first, the Rogan-Gladen estimator plugs in point estimates of $\widehat{\text{Sp}}_{<5.0} = 1.00$ and $\widehat{\text{Se}}_{<5.0}$ without propagating binomial sampling uncertainty from the $N_A = 44\text{--}50$ and $N_B = 50\text{--}51$ calibration cohorts; second, filtering for `OPEN_ACCESS:y AND IN_EPMC:y` over-represents commercial gold open-access megajournals ($159/320 = 49.7\%$ of the sample) relative to subscription or hybrid society journals that do not deposit full-text XML in Europe PMC.

* **Claim 3 — Peer review at selective flagship and society journals improves manuscripts more than twice as much as at megajournals ($+0.38$ vs. $+0.18$ $\text{Core}_8$ points), and BARS recalls 97.9% ($95/97$) of unresolved human critiques while catching 385 additional flaws: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The paired version-1 preprint-to-published design ($n = 25$ selective vs. $n = 25$ megajournal pairs, blinded to version and venue) and the concrete examples of revision-induced errors (`PAIR_31`, `PAIR_38`, `PAIR_39`) provide direct evidence that selective peer review adds measurable technical value while $36\%$ ($9/25$) of megajournal pairs declined slightly after revision. In the 100-paper open-review audit, the 385 BARS-only catches were checked for token/accession existence in the source text via `scripts/verify_review_facts.py`. However, Stage 2 concordance grading—determining whether Stage 1 `gemini-4-argon` reviews matched the 363 human referee points and classifying referee anomalies—was performed by `gemini-4-argon` itself (AI Disclosure Statement, line 248) without an independent human-annotated inter-rater reliability check.

* **Claim 4 — Across 915 date-blinded papers from 1991 to 2025, average quality fell from $7.6/10$ to $5.8/10$ primarily due to compositional shifts toward commercial megajournals and secondary database templates: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The within-journal fixed-effect regression across 30 recurring multi-year journals ($n = 549$, $\beta = -0.23$ points/decade, $p = 0.027$) and the Oaxaca–Blinder decomposition ($54.1\%\text{--}58.3\%$ compositional) support the conclusion that ecosystem expansion rather than within-journal deterioration drove most of the observed drop. However, two methodological issues affect the 35-year analysis: (i) in Experiment 5, the XML parser extracted only `<body>` and omitted `<back>` and `<custom-meta>` (Methods line 238, Supplementary Section S2 line 321), stripping modern `Data Availability` sections while the main text and Figure 4A headline the 10-point composite score (which includes the 2.0-point Reproducibility/Data Availability component); and (ii) Europe PMC `OPEN_ACCESS:y` XML deposits in 1991–2000 almost exclusively reflect retrospective digitization of elite society and university-press journals, creating severe baseline ascertainment bias when compared against 2020s gold open-access XML deposits.

* **Claim 5 — Research quality peaks at 2–3 NIH grants ($\sim\$0.95\text{M/year}$, $7.63/10$), unfunded/small dry-lab studies ($<\$25\text{k}$ and $\$25\text{k--}\$100\text{k}$) average $5.24\text{--}5.25/10$, and $\$3,242$ society journals outperform $\$2,879$ commercial megajournals by $+0.72$ points: [Fragile].**  
  The comparisons across verified NIH RePORTER award brackets ($n = 222$ NIH-funded papers) and journal APC tiers ($N = 1,076$) are grounded in external metadata. By contrast, the "Estimated Study Cost" analysis (`Est. Study Cost (N=1,076)` in Table S6 lines 577–581 and main text line 167, reporting exact group medians such as $\$14,880$ for $n = 138$ studies $<\$25\text{k}$ and $\$72,224$ for $n = 162$ studies in $\$25\text{k--}\$100\text{k}$) is never defined in the Methods or Supplementary Information, even though 854 of the 1,076 papers have $\$0$ NIH funding and 615 acknowledge zero grants of any kind. Imputing study production costs from manuscript modality or assay descriptions and then correlating those imputed cost brackets with BARS scores introduces circularity.

* **Claim 6 — Blinded career self-audits of 238 papers by two senior authors—and of this manuscript itself—expose real mathematical, control, and table errors in the authors' own work: [Overstated].**  
  The career self-audits of `[SENIOR_AUTHOR_1]` ($N = 164$) and `[SENIOR_AUTHOR_2]` ($N = 74$) are candid and well documented in Table S5, detailing specific table bugs, unit errors, and template leakage in their own lead- and middle-author papers. However, the claim that *this manuscript itself* was audited under the same BARS pipeline—featured in the Abstract (line 7), Table 1 (line 39), the Results section heading (line 132), line 152, the Discussion (line 191), and the Methods (line 235)—is left incomplete: line 152 states *"Finally, we subjected a de-identified draft of this manuscript and its Supplementary Information to the same BARS pipeline"* and immediately ends the section without reporting the score, sub-scores, or errors caught in this manuscript.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Pre-Relabeling Calibration Specificity in the Main Text and Propagate Uncertainty Into Prevalence Estimates
* **WHY:** Moving `PMC8135569` ($4.2/10$) from Cohort B to Cohort A after the Phase-1 Pilot converts Cohort B specificity at $<5.0$ from $98.0\%$ ($50/51$) to $100.0\%$ ($50/50$). Treating $\widehat{\text{Sp}} = 1.00$ and $\widehat{\text{Se}}$ as exact constants in the Rogan-Gladen estimator (Eq. 3) overstates the precision of the inferred $60\%\text{--}70\%$ defect prevalence.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state the `PMC8135569` pilot relabeling in Results (`Take-Home 1`) alongside the $98.0\%$ ($50/51$) unedited specificity, and report $95\%$ confidence intervals for $\widehat{\text{Se}}$, $\widehat{\text{Sp}}$, and $\hat{\pi}_{\text{RG}}$.
* **HOW:** In `scripts/verify_repeatability_and_calibration.py`, compute Clopper-Pearson exact $95\%$ intervals for $\widehat{\text{Se}}$ and $\widehat{\text{Sp}}$ under both the $50/50$ and $50/51$ Cohort B definitions, and run a 10,000-draw nonparametric bootstrap over $(N_A, N_B, N_{\text{wild}})$ in Eq. 3 to report $\hat{\pi}_{\text{RG}}$ with $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals in `Take-Home 2` and Table S1.

#### 2. Standardize JATS XML Section Parsing Across Cohorts or Anchor Temporal and Self-Audit Plots on $\text{Core}_8$
* **WHY:** Experiments 5 ($N = 915$ secular trend) and 6a (`[SENIOR_AUTHOR_1]` self-audit, $N = 164$) parsed only `<body>` XML, omitting `<back>` and `<custom-meta>` where modern journals place `Data Availability` and `Code Availability` statements, whereas Experiments 4 and 6b extracted `<back>` and `<custom-meta>`. Grading `<back>`-stripped manuscripts on the 10-point scale penalizes modern articles on the 2.0-point Reproducibility sub-score and creates a parser asymmetry between `[SENIOR_AUTHOR_1]` (Experiment 6a) and `[SENIOR_AUTHOR_2]` (Experiment 6b).
* **WHAT:** Either re-score the affected modern manuscripts in Experiments 5 and 6a using the unified JATS extractor (`<body> + <back> + <custom-meta>`) or use the 8-point Reproducibility-free Scientific Core ($\text{Core}_8$, scaled to $/10$ if desired) as the primary metric in Figures 4, 5, and 6.
* **HOW:** Extract `<back>` and `<custom-meta>` for the Experiment 5 and 6a XML files via `scripts/build_deidentified.py` to check how many papers had Data Availability statements omitted, and update Figure 4A/B and Figure 5A/B to display $\text{Core}_8$ alongside $S_{\text{total}}$.

#### 3. Add a Human-Annotated Audit of Stage 2 Concordance and Document or Remove Imputed Study Costs
* **WHY:** In Experiment 4, `gemini-4-argon` evaluated its own Stage 1 recall against human referees ($97.9\%$). In Experiment 6 (Economics, Table S6), `Est. Study Cost (N=1,076)` is reported for 854 non-NIH papers without any methodological definition.
* **WHAT:** Validate Stage 2 concordance on a random subset of open-review papers with two independent human raters, and either fully document the `Est. Study Cost` estimation formula in Methods (and test for circularity against modality covariates) or remove the imputed cost rows (`lines 577–581`) in favor of the empirical NIH RePORTER grant brackets (`lines 564–575`).
* **HOW:** Have the authors independently score 20 randomly selected papers from `OPEN_ANON_001`–`100` (~70 human critiques and ~75 BARS-only catches) to report human-vs.-LLM Cohen's $\kappa$ on Stage 2 recall and false-positive catch rates. In `Take-Home 6` and Table S6, restrict primary funding claims to verified NIH RePORTER award totals and raw acknowledged grant counts, and deduplicate the 29 overlapping natural-replicate papers across the $N = 1,076$ pooled regressions.

#### 4. Complete the Missing Manuscript Self-Audit (`MANUSCRIPT_META_01`) and Temper Abstract Generalizations
* **WHY:** Six places in the manuscript (lines 7, 39, 132, 152, 191, and 235) claim that a blinded draft of this paper was audited by BARS and caught real errors, but line 152 stops before presenting the results. Furthermore, the Abstract presents the 1991–2025 decline ($7.6/10$ to $5.8/10$) and $54\%$ failure rate without noting the Europe PMC open-access XML sampling frame.
* **WHAT:** Add the complete quantitative scorecard and specific errors caught during the blinded self-audit of `MANUSCRIPT_META_01` after line 152 and in Table S5, and specify in the Abstract that the 320-paper and 915-paper cohorts sample open-access full texts in Europe PMC.
* **HOW:** Insert a dedicated bullet or paragraph at line 152 reporting the blinded score breakdown (`Rigor`, `Claims`, `Biology`, `Reproducibility`) of `MANUSCRIPT_META_01` and listing the exact statistical, table, or framing flaws that BARS flagged in the draft and how they were fixed.
