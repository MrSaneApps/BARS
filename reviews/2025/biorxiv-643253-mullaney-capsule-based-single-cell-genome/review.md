### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript characterizes a commercially available semi-permeable capsule (SPC) composed of an acrylate-substituted polysaccharide hydrogel shell and a dextran core (`Atrandi Biosciences`), generated via co-flow droplet microfluidics and cleavable on demand by glycosidase treatment. The authors evaluate SPC stability across extreme chemical and thermal exposures (`6 M HCl` to `6 M NaOH`, organic solvents, `-196 °C` to `100 °C`), calibrate size-selective permeability using DNA ladders (`10–10,000 bp`), native protein standards (`15–600 kDa`), ribosomes, mononucleosomes, and de novo designed protein oligomers (`6-mer` to `60-mer`), and demonstrate four downstream applications: (1) high-throughput microbial and mammalian cell culture (`> 500,000` occupied capsules), (2) in-capsule PCR and isothermal primary template amplification (PTA), (3) 4-round ligation-based combinatorial indexing (`24⁴` barcode combinations) for targeted 4-gene single-cell amplicon sequencing (`387` cells) and human-mouse barnyard single-cell whole-genome sequencing (sc-WGS) comparing modified multiple displacement amplification (mMDA) and PTA, and (4) proof-of-concept lineage tracing across `1,000` serially bottlenecked K562 cells engineered with the hypermutator polymerase ε allele (`POLE P286R`). While wet-lab protocols are thoroughly detailed in the Methods (pp. 32–48), the `Data Availability` section (p. 26) is blank, with no public repository accessions provided for any of the raw sequencing datasets.

The physical and biochemical characterization of the polysaccharide SPC platform and its integration with split-pool ligation barcoding for targeted single-cell amplicons represent a practical methodological contribution. However, several quantitative claims in the mammalian cell culture, sc-WGS coverage uniformity, and `POLE P286R` lineage tracing sections rest on flawed statistical procedures or internal contradictions. Specifically: (1) the claim of culturing "clonally isolated" mammalian cells conflicts with the data showing that single encapsulated K562 cells die (`72%` viability loss by day 4, near-total death by day 8) and that sustained growth required high-density encapsulation (`69.16%` occupancy, `32.9%` multiplet rate) in conditioned media, which selects for capsules founded by multiple cells; (2) the reported sc-WGS coverage uniformity (`Gini = 0.236` for mMDA and `0.277` for PTA) is computed after excluding all zero-coverage `1 kb` bins at shallow sequencing depth (`mean = 1,433,349` reads/cell, or `< 0.5` reads per `1 kb` bin), a censoring step that erases whole-genome amplification (WGA) locus dropout; and (3) the `POLE P286R` lineage tracing experiment was initiated from polyclonal bottlenecks (`3–5` cells rather than single cells), removes variants called as heterozygous in the pooled pseudo-bulk (which inadvertently strips early lineage-defining somatic mutations), contains an arithmetic impossibility between total variants (`1,379,244`) and per-cell average (`954` variants/cell across `1,000` cells), and builds uncalibrated UPGMA trees from sparse pairwise site overlaps without uncertainty propagation. These issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis of the existing sequencing data, honest reporting of zero-coverage dropout and barnyard doublet rates, and tempering of the mammalian clonality and lineage-tracing claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Polysaccharide SPCs are physically and chemically stable across extreme pH, solvent, and thermal conditions while maintaining sharp size-selective permeability (`> 300 bp` DNA retained, `< 300 kDa` globular proteins exchanged): [Well Supported].**  
  The stability assays (Fig. S1), gel retention time-courses for DNA ladders (`< 100 bp` lost rapidly, `100–300 bp` lost at `5.1%` per wash, `> 300 bp` fully retained; Fig. 1D/E, Fig. S3A), native proteins, mononucleosomes (`46.8%` retained), ribosomes (`47.3%` retained), atomically precise de novo protein oligomers (`6-mer` to `60-mer` retained over 10 days; Fig. 1E/F, Figs. S3E–G, S4), and anti-CD19-PE IgG influx into encapsulated Nalm6 cells (Fig. 1G, Fig. S5) directly establish the molecular weight and hydrodynamic cutoff of the hydrogel shell.

* **SPCs support high-throughput clonal bacterial culture, whereas sustained mammalian cell proliferation in SPCs requires multi-cell loading and conditioned media rather than single-cell clonal isolation: [Overstated].**  
  For *E. coli*, Poisson loading at `21%` occupancy (`525,000` loaded SPCs, `2.37%` multiplet rate) yielded rapid growth (`20 min` doubling time vs. `29 min` in suspension; Fig. 2B) and expected clonal segregation in a 50:50 eGFP/mScarlet mixing assay (`13.8%` eGFP+, `11.3%` mScarlet+, `3.2%` mixed; Fig. 2C,D). In contrast, K562 cells loaded at `44.5%` occupancy (`11.8%` multiplet rate) in fresh media died off by day 8 (p. 10, Fig. S6A). Proliferation of K562, Jurkat, Nalm6, and HEK293T cells required high-density loading (`69.16%` occupancy, `32.9%` multiplet rate; p. 10, p. 36) in conditioned media, and all lines exhibited an initial drop in cell counts that the authors acknowledge *"reflects the death of single cells isolated within SPCs and the selection for cells within SPCs containing multiple cells"* (p. 10). Furthermore, Fig. S6A confounds loading density with conditioned media for K562 (`low density in fresh media` vs. `high density in conditioned media`). Describing the mammalian assays as *"clonally isolated cellular cultures"* (p. 2, p. 11) is therefore unsupported by the experimental design.

* **Ligation-based combinatorial indexing of targeted multiplex PCR amplicons (`> 400 bp`) inside SPCs achieves high single-cell sensitivity and low cross-capsule allele swapping: [Well Supported].**  
  Despite showing that unanchored PCR amplicons (`100–1,500 bp`) and PTA products leak into the supernatant and that `100–500 bp` amplicons can diffuse into empty SPCs during bulk cycling (Figs. S10–S12), the 4-gene multiplex amplicon assay (`AKT3`, `BIRC6`, `FANCC`, `BRCA1`; `460–2,155 bp`, 16 PCR cycles) across `387` K562 and PBMC cells (`mean = 1,008` reads/cell) detected all four targets in `96.9%` of cells (Fig. 3D), achieved `99.2%` (`39` cells, intact) and `100%` (`20` cells, fragmented) allele purity at the homozygous `FANCC` SNP (`chr9:95,247,502`) in 10:90 and 20:80 mixtures (Fig. 3E), and recovered both alleles of the heterozygous `AKT3 p.G37Ter` variant (`chr1:243,695,654`) in `84.91%` of K562 cells (`mean = 28.34` reads/cell; Fig. 3F).

* **SPC-based sc-WGS with mMDA and PTA yields highly uniform genome-wide coverage (`Gini = 0.236` and `0.277`) with minimal cross-contamination: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the Gini coefficients (`0.236` for mMDA, `0.277` for PTA; p. 19, Fig. 3I) were computed in `1 kb` bins after **excluding all zero-coverage bins** (p. 44: *"To account for regions lacking coverage, bins with zero coverage were excluded"*). At a shallow mean sequencing depth of `1,433,349` reads/cell across a `~3.0 × 10⁶ kb` mammalian genome (`< 0.5` reads per `1 kb` bin on average), the majority of `1 kb` bins have zero reads due to both Poisson sampling and WGA locus dropout. Censoring zero-coverage bins removes WGA dropout entirely and restricts the Lorenz curve to the small fraction of bins with `≥ 1` read (most having `1` or `2` reads), yielding artificially low Gini values that cannot distinguish true amplification uniformity from severe locus dropout. Second, while Fig. 3G plots human vs. mouse read counts, the manuscript never reports the total number of sequenced cells, the empirical human-mouse doublet rate versus Poisson expectation, or the quantitative cross-species contamination fraction (median and IQR of exogenous species reads per cell) for mMDA versus PTA.

* **The `POLE P286R` allele provides a constant, quantitative molecular clock (`862` mutations per million reads per day, `r = 0.58`) that faithfully reconstructs single-cell lineages at shallow sc-WGS depth: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Multiple experimental and statistical flaws undermine this section (pp. 21–24, 44–48):
  1. *Bulk WGS VAF detection limit vs. de novo single-cell division marks (Fig. 4B):* In bulk WGS of `1.5 × 10⁵` to `1.0 × 10⁶` cycling cells (p. 45), sites were downsampled to `10` reads and called as SNPs only if `≥ 2` reads supported the alternate allele (`VAF ≥ 20%`). Neutral mutations arising during individual cell divisions in a population of `> 10⁵` cells have `VAF ≈ 10⁻⁶` and are undetectable at a `10`-read depth threshold; variants reaching `≥ 2` of `10` reads in bulk culture necessarily represent pre-existing heterogeneity or clonal sweeps rather than steady single-cell division ticking, and the linear fit explains only `34%` of variance (`r = 0.58`, `R² = 0.34`, with no `p`-value or slope confidence interval reported).
  2. *Internal numerical contradiction in variant counts (p. 22, Fig. 4F):* The text reports *"a total of 1,379,244 detectable variants with 2 or more reads supporting the alternative allele, and an average of 954 variants per cell"* across `1,000` single cells. However, `1,000` cells with a mean of `954` variants/cell contain at most `1,000 × 954 = 954,000` total cell-variant observations (even if `100%` of variants were unique singletons). Reporting `1,379,244` unique variants (`AD ≥ 2`) is mathematically impossible unless the cell count exceeds `1,445` or the `1,379,244` count includes `AD = 1` calls or unbarcoded/empty-capsule reads.
  3. *Polyclonal founding (`3–5` cells) and circular pseudo-bulk germline filtering (p. 22, p. 45, p. 48):* The bottleneck experiment was initiated by plating `1,000` edited cells across two 96-well plates (`~5.2` cells/well; p. 22 states `"starting with approximately 3–5 cells"`). Because bulk editing efficiency was `~50%` (Fig. S14), a `3–5` cell founding population is polyclonal and potentially heterogeneous for `POLE P286R` zygosity. Thus, shifts in subpopulation frequencies across serial 1:1 splits (such as the discordant placement of population `P2` in Fig. 4G) can arise simply from sorting of the `3–5` founder lineages rather than de novo mutational divergence. Furthermore, removing all sites called as heterozygous or homozygous alternate in the pooled ("bulked") sc-WGS dataset as "K562 germline variants" (p. 48) removes genuine early somatic mutations that arose in the founder clone prior to Day 9 or during the earliest splits (`P0`/`P1`).
  4. *Sparse pairwise site intersections and unvalidated UPGMA clustering (p. 48, Fig. 4G,H):* Pairwise Hamming distances between single cells were computed over sites covered by `> 2` reads (`≥ 3` reads) in *both* cells. At shallow sequencing depth (`~0.1–0.2×` genome coverage), the intersection of `≥ 3`-read sites between two cells is tiny and varies widely across pairs, while binomial allelic dropout for a heterozygous variant (`AD ≥ 2` at depth `3`) is `50%`. Constructing a UPGMA tree from non-metric, variable-denominator pairwise distances without reporting pairwise site overlap counts, bootstrap support, or tree accuracy metrics (`Robinson-Foulds` or cophenetic correlation) does not establish quantitative single-cell lineage reconstruction.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Recompute sc-WGS Coverage Uniformity (Lorenz Curves and Gini Coefficients) Without Censoring Zero-Coverage Bins and Report Barnyard Cross-Contamination Metrics
* **WHY:** Excluding zero-coverage `1 kb` bins at `< 0.5` reads/bin (p. 44) removes WGA locus dropout—the primary failure mode of MDA-family chemistry—and compresses Gini coefficients (`0.236` vs. `0.277`) into an artifact of integer read counts (`1` vs. `2` reads/bin). Additionally, qualitative inspection of Fig. 3G is insufficient to establish low cross-capsule contamination given the DNA leakage documented in Figs. S10–S12.
* **WHAT:** Recompute Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients across all mappable autosomal bins (including zero-coverage bins) at matched, downsampled per-cell read depth, and report quantitative barnyard purity and multiplet statistics in the text and Fig. 3.
* **HOW:** 
  1. Filter the hg38/mm10 reference genomes to mappable, non-N autosomal windows at `10 kb`, `50 kb`, and `100 kb` resolution (where expected read depth per bin is `≥ 5–50` reads).
  2. Downsample all cells passing quality control in the mMDA and PTA libraries to a fixed read count (e.g., `500,000` or `1,000,000` uniquely mapped reads/cell) and compute the Gini coefficient **including** `0`-read mappable bins, alongside the theoretical Poisson baseline Gini at that exact read depth and the genome breadth of coverage (`%` of genome covered at `≥ 1` read) as a function of total reads.
  3. For Fig. 3G, explicitly report in the text: (a) total cells recovered for mMDA and PTA, (b) observed human-mouse doublet rate (`%` of barcodes with `> 5%` or `> 10%` reads from both species) compared against the Poisson prediction from `250,000` input cells, and (c) the median, mean, and IQR of cross-species contaminating read fractions in single-species cells.

#### 2. Audit and Correct the `POLE P286R` Variant Matrix, Germline Filter, and Phylogenetic Reconstruction
* **WHY:** The reported `1,379,244` total variants (`AD ≥ 2`) versus `954` average variants/cell across `1,000` cells is mathematically inconsistent (`1,000 × 954 = 954,000 < 1,379,244`). Furthermore, filtering out variants called as heterozygous in the pseudo-bulk of `P0–P5` strips shared early somatic mutations, while UPGMA on sparse `≥ 3`-read pairwise intersections conflates coverage dropout with evolutionary distance.
* **WHAT:** Reconcile the variant counts, replace the circular pseudo-bulk germline filter with an external unedited K562 control filter, quantify pairwise site overlap and tree support, and verify `POLE P286R` allele frequency across `P0–P5`.
* **HOW:**
  1. Check the `anndata` object creation script (p. 47–48) to determine why the primary layer (`AD ≥ 1` boolean matrix, line 1526) and the `AD ≥ 2` filter (Fig. 4F) produced `1,379,244` total sites vs. `954` sites/cell, and report the exact number of cells (`N`), total unique variant sites at `AD ≥ 2`, and total shared (`≥ 2` cells) variant sites at `AD ≥ 2`.
  2. Instead of removing heterozygous sites called in the pooled `P0–P5` sc-WGS reads, filter pre-existing K562 germline/background variants using the Day 0 / non-targeting control bulk WGS dataset (already sequenced for Fig. 4B) plus `gnomAD` / common K562 variant calls, thereby preserving early trunk and clade-defining mutations acquired between Days 1 and 19.
  3. Report the distribution (median and IQR) of the number of co-covered sites (`> 2` reads in both cells) across all `499,500` cell pairs in Fig. 4H, impose a minimum shared-site denominator threshold (e.g., `≥ 500` shared informative sites per pair), and evaluate phylogenetic concordance against the known `P0–P5` split topology using `Robinson-Foulds` distance, cophenetic correlation, and `1,000` bootstrap replicates (comparing UPGMA against neighbor-joining which does not assume strict ultrametricity).
  4. Check sequencing reads mapping to the `POLE P286R` locus (or the `TCT>TTT` trinucleotide mutation proportion) specifically within Population `P2` vs. `P0, P1, P3–P5` to test whether `P2`'s lower mutation count (`Fig. 4F`) and topological discordance (`Fig. 4G`) resulted from outgrowth of an unedited or wild-type cell from the `3–5` cell founding bottleneck.

#### 3. Decouple Loading Density from Conditioned Media in Mammalian SPC Culture and Reframe Clonality Claims
* **WHY:** Single mammalian cells died in fresh media (`44.5%` occupancy, `11.8%` multiplet rate), and viable growth was only shown at `69.16%` occupancy (`32.9%` multiplet rate) in conditioned media, accompanied by early cell death that selects for multi-cell capsules.
* **WHAT:** Either add a sub-Poisson conditioned-media control (`≤ 10–15%` occupancy) tracking individual single-cell-occupied capsules by time-lapse imaging, or revise the Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion to distinguish clonal bacterial culture from multi-cell spheroid/colony mammalian culture.
* **HOW:** Without generating new wet-lab data, revise the Abstract (p. 1, line 17), Introduction (p. 2, line 59), Results (p. 11, line 297), and Discussion (p. 25, line 637) to state explicitly that *E. coli* proliferates clonally from single cells in SPCs, whereas mammalian cell lines (`K562`, `Jurkat`, `Nalm6`, `HEK293T`) require paracrine signaling (conditioned media and/or multi-cell co-encapsulation at `≥ 33%` multiplet rates) to form 3D colonies and spheroids.

#### 4. Clarify Bulk WGS Mutation Rate Interpretation and Report Full Regression Statistics (Figure 4B)
* **WHY:** In bulk WGS of `1.5 × 10⁵` to `1.0 × 10⁶` cells downsampled to `10` reads with an `AD ≥ 2` cutoff (`VAF ≥ 20%`), newly arising single-cell mutations (`VAF ≈ 10⁻⁶`) are invisible; thus, the slope of `862` mutations per million reads per day (`r = 0.58`) measures the accumulation of high-frequency subclonal variants rather than the per-division single-cell mutation rate.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact regression statistics (`slope ± 95% CI`, `R²`, `p`-value, and sample size `n`) for both total mutations and `TCT>TTT` mutations in Fig. 4B, and explain the VAF detection limit of bulk `10`-read downsampled WGS versus single-cell WGS.
* **HOW:** From the `scipy.statsmodels` output (p. 45), add the `95% CI` and two-sided `p`-value for the linear regression slopes in Fig. 4B (left and right panels), and note in the text (p. 22) that bulk sequencing at `10×` downsampled depth captures drift/expansion of early post-editing mutations (`VAF ≥ 20%`), motivating the shift to sc-WGS in Fig. 4D–H to resolve low-frequency somatic variants in individual cells.

#### 5. Populate Data Availability and Fix Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Inconsistencies
* **WHY:** A blank `Data Availability` section, order-of-magnitude unit errors in the Methods, conflicting capsule sizes, and mislabeled figure panels impede reproducibility and reader verification.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Data & Code Availability (p. 26):** Deposit raw FASTQ/BAM files for all four sequencing experiments (targeted 4-gene amplicon barcoding, human-mouse barnyard mMDA/PTA sc-WGS, bulk `POLE P286R` WGS time-course, and `1,000`-cell `POLE P286R` sc-WGS) in `NCBI SRA` / `GEO` and insert the accession numbers on p. 26. Include the custom Python scripts for Levenshtein barcode demultiplexing (p. 42), Lorenz/Gini calculation (p. 44), and `anndata` UPGMA lineage construction (p. 47–48), as well as the atomic coordinates (`.pdb` file) for the newly designed de novo protein oligomer (p. 4, p. 35).
  2. **Capsule Diameter Discrepancy (p. 3 vs. p. 32–46):** Reconcile the main text statement that experiments primarily used SPCs with a mean diameter of `82.9 µm` (`SD = 6.33 µm`, plus `40.4 µm` and `182.6 µm` in Fig. 1B) with the Methods (p. 32 Table S1, pp. 34, 35, 37, 46), which repeatedly state that MCN-C2, MCN-C4, and CCN-G4-Q2 generate `35 µm`, `65 µm`, and `175 µm` SPCs (likely reflecting nominal droplet size in oil prior to aqueous swelling upon shell hydration).

Correct both numbers in the Methods.
  4. **Reagent Catalog Number Mix-up (p. 32 vs. pp. 33, 36, 38, 39):** On p. 32 (line 698) and p. 36 (line 903), `CRP-PA1` is the Photoinitiator, whereas on p. 33 (line 786), p. 36 (line 913), p. 38 (line 1039), and p. 39 (line 1068), the glycosidase Release Agent is erroneously listed as `CRP-PA1` instead of `CRP-RR1` (used on pp. 40, 43, 47).
